# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Individuals (25–45) managing their personal finances across chequing, savings, TFSA, RRSP, FHSA, and credit accounts. They return daily to check balances and monthly to review budgets and net worth. Financial data carries emotional weight — they want clarity and control, not judgment or overwhelm.

## Product Purpose

Bloom is a personal banking dashboard that gives users a clear, real-time picture of their financial life. Success looks like: a user knows their net worth at a glance, trusts their budget numbers, and feels in control — not anxious.

## Brand Personality

Sharp · Minimal · Focused

The interface is a precision tool. It never decorates for its own sake, never hypes, never celebrates what doesn't warrant it. It surfaces the signal and gets out of the way.

## Anti-references

- Purple or teal gradients
- Glassmorphism cards
- Confetti or celebration animations on milestones
- Generic blue SaaS primary buttons
- Illustrated empty states with cartoon characters
- Gratuitous pulsing or looping animations
- Emoji-heavy UI
- "Boost your savings!" motivational CTA language
- Bright neon greens as "positive money" signals

## Design Principles

1. **Numbers first** — financial data is the content; UI chrome is infrastructure. It should be invisible when it's working.
2. **One signal at a time** — never compete for attention. Each screen surfaces what matters now; everything else recedes.
3. **Calm on bad news** — the interface never catastrophizes. Negative net worth or overspent budgets are shown clearly, not dramatized.
4. **Precision over decoration** — every visual decision earns its place through function. If removing it doesn't change behavior, remove it.
5. **Trust through consistency** — a user who sees the same interaction pattern twice builds trust faster than one who sees twelve clever variants.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA. Body text 4.5:1 contrast minimum against background. Large/bold text 3:1 minimum. Full keyboard navigation. Reduced motion should be respected where animations are present.
